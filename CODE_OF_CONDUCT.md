# Code of conduct

## What this is

A short statement of how people are expected to treat each other in this
repository's issues, discussions, and pull requests. It is not a legal document
and there is no committee behind it; the maintainers apply it directly.

## Expected behaviour

- Discuss code and decisions, not people. "This allocates on the paint path" is
  useful; "you clearly do not understand performance" is not.
- Assume the other person is trying to make the project better. Most disagreements
  here are about a tradeoff, and a tradeoff stated plainly is easier to settle than
  one argued about.
- Say what you measured. A claim about speed, size, or platform behaviour carries
  much more weight with the machine, the build mode, and the method attached.
- Accept that a maintainer may say no. Viva has a stated scope, a set of
  performance budgets, and a framework-versus-application boundary; a change can be
  declined because it does not fit them, and that is not a judgement about you.
- Be patient with people working in a second language, on an unfamiliar platform,
  or in a timezone that is not yours.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, and personal attacks, in public or in private.
- Discriminatory comments about who someone is.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of that kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their consent.
- Deliberately wasting reviewers' time: argument for its own sake, or reopening a
  settled decision without new information.
- Sustained disruption of a discussion after being asked to stop.

## Scope

This applies in the repository's issues, pull requests, discussions, and commit
messages, and in any space where someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Tell a maintainer privately — through their GitHub profile rather than a public
thread. Reports are handled as confidentially as the situation allows.

Maintainers may edit or delete a comment, close a thread, or block an account, in
proportion to what happened. Someone asked to stop a behaviour is expected to stop
it; continuing is treated as a separate problem.
